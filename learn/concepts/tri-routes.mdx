---
title: "Tri routes"
description: "Three-venue execution routes."
---

A tri route splits one trade across three venues.

Tri routes can unlock deeper liquidity than single or pair routes, but they add coordination complexity. Lotus should only use them when the math clearly improves the user's executable result.

![Routing decision flow](/images/learn/routing-decision-flow.svg)

## When tri routes help

Tri routes are most useful when:

- A user trades a larger notional size.
- No single venue has enough depth.
- A pair route still leaves meaningful slippage.
- A third venue improves the blended price after fees.
- All three venues pass routeability checks.

## Why tri routes are stricter

More legs mean more things can fail.

A tri route needs:

- Three compatible venue legs.
- Three fresh executable quotes.
- Three valid venue/outcome mappings.
- Enough user balance or sellable position per leg.
- Supported signing and submit paths.
- Settlement tracking for every leg.

If any leg is unsafe, Lotus should not force the tri route.

## Tri route vs. pair route

Lotus should compare the tri route against the best pair route, not only against a single venue.

The tri route wins only when it strictly improves the expected outcome after fees, slippage, and risk. If a pair route is almost as good with less complexity, the pair route may be better.

## User-facing result

Users should not need to manage three venues manually.

They should see one route preview, one Place action, one signing flow if required, and one final execution receipt that explains each leg.
