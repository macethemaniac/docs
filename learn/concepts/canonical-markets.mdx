---
title: "Canonical markets"
description: "One market across many venues."
---

A canonical market is Lotus's normalized representation of the same tradeable outcome across venues.

Users trade the canonical market. Lotus routes to the underlying venues.

## Example

Several venues may list a market like:

> Will BTC reach $150,000 by December 31?

The titles may look similar, but Lotus cannot assume they are the same market. One venue may resolve from a different price source. Another may use a different timestamp. Another may include edge cases that change how the market settles.

Lotus only groups venue markets when the routeable overlap is clear.

## What canonicalization checks

Canonicalization combines:

1. [Shared outcome core](/learn/concepts/shared-outcome-core): the outcome that all included venues agree on.
2. [Rule compatibility](/learn/concepts/rule-compatibility): whether venue resolution rules are equivalent enough to route.
3. Time alignment: whether the resolution window matches.
4. Outcome mapping: whether the same outcome can be identified across venues.
5. Venue metadata quality: whether Lotus has enough trusted venue-sourced data to justify the match.

## What a canonical market contains

A canonical market can include:

- One canonical event.
- One or more canonical outcomes.
- One or more venue markets.
- Venue-specific market IDs and outcome IDs.
- Resolution rules and source metadata.
- Display prices from live snapshots.
- Routeability metadata for execution.

## Visible does not always mean executable

A market can be visible before every venue is executable.

For display, Lotus can show the best trusted live price it has. For execution, Lotus requires stricter checks: fresh quotes, valid venue outcome IDs, venue readiness, user balance, signer readiness, route approval, and settlement safety.

<Note>
  Display data helps users understand the market. Execution authority comes only from fresh backend quote and readiness checks.
</Note>

## Why canonical markets matter

Canonical markets let users and builders think in terms of the outcome they want, not the venue that happens to list it.

They also give Lotus the structure needed to compare prices, build pair and tri routes, send RFQs, and reconcile positions after execution.
