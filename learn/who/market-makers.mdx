Market makers can supply liquidity to Lotus through structured RFQ flow.

Instead of waiting for users to trade on one venue at a time, LPs can receive canonical market demand from Lotus and compete to improve execution.

## What market makers get

Lotus gives LPs:

- Structured RFQ sessions.
- Canonical market context.
- Quote submission APIs.
- Authenticated quote intake.
- Quote expiry controls.
- Resolution lane checks.
- Route selection against venue books and competing quotes.
- Fill and execution status events.

## How LP quoting works

An eligible LP receives or discovers an RFQ session, then submits a quote with price, quantity, fee, and expiry.

Lotus validates the request before accepting the quote:

- The LP API key must be active.
- The HMAC signature must match the request.
- The timestamp must be inside the allowed clock skew.
- The nonce must not be replayed.
- The LP route ID must match the authenticated LP identity.
- The RFQ session must still be collecting quotes.
- The quote ID must not be duplicated.

Accepted quotes enter the router. They only win when they improve the route and satisfy Lotus safety rules.

## Why quote through Lotus

Lotus lets LPs compete for cross-venue demand without building every venue integration or every frontend partnership.

LPs can focus on pricing, inventory, and risk. Lotus handles canonicalization, routeability, user execution flow, settlement checks, and position accounting.

## What a winning quote means

A winning quote is not simply the cheapest visible quote.

Lotus compares:

- Price.
- Quantity.
- Fees.
- Expiry.
- Fill confidence.
- Resolution compatibility.
- Route risk.
- User slippage tolerance.

The quote must improve the user's executable route.

## Integration path

The current LP path supports authenticated quote submission. The builder-ready path should add:

- Open RFQ discovery.
- RFQ WebSocket streams.
- Quote amend and cancel endpoints.
- LP stats.
- API key management.
- SDK signing helpers.
- Clear rate limits.
- Full error examples.
