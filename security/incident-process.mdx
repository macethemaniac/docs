---
title: "Incident process"
description: "How Lotus responds to security and execution incidents."
---

Lotus treats security incidents, execution mismatches, and settlement anomalies as production safety events.

This public process explains the phases without exposing private runbooks or privileged commands.

## Incident phases

<Steps>
  <Step title="Detect">
    Lotus monitors execution state, settlement verification, funding readiness, error rates, and venue health signals.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Triage">
    Operators classify severity, affected users, affected venues, and whether execution should be paused or limited.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contain">
    Lotus can fail closed, pause affected routes, disable unsafe venues, or restrict specific execution paths.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reconcile">
    The team verifies fills, positions, balances, receipts, and fee ledger entries.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Communicate">
    Users and builders receive clear status updates when they are affected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review">
    Lotus records root cause, impact, mitigation, and follow-up work.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Public communication

Public updates should include:

- What happened.
- Who was affected.
- User-visible impact.
- Current status.
- Mitigation taken.
- Follow-up action.

Public updates should not include:

- Secrets.
- Private infrastructure details.
- Exploit payloads.
- Privileged recovery commands.
- Internal detection thresholds.

## User protection

During an incident, Lotus should prioritize:

- Preventing unsafe new execution.
- Reconciling real venue fills.
- Preserving user balances and positions.
- Making receipts accurate.
- Communicating clearly.

<Note>
  Incident transparency should explain impact and controls without exposing material that increases future risk.
</Note>
