---
title: "Understanding surplus capture"
description: "How Lotus accounts for price improvement and share improvement beyond the baseline route."
---

Surplus is the improvement produced by a Lotus route compared with a reference baseline.

For a trader, surplus is the extra value created by better routing. Lotus earns by capturing a transparent share of that value — only when execution actually improves the outcome.

Lotus recognizes two distinct types of improvement:

- **Price improvement** — a better average execution price per share.
- **Share improvement** — more shares filled than any single venue could supply alone.

## What creates price improvement

Price improvement can come from:

- Better venue depth.
- Multi-venue splitting that achieves a lower blended average price.
- RFQ liquidity at better prices.
- Lower slippage from deeper orderbooks.
- Faster execution against fresh quotes.

## What creates share improvement

Share improvement comes from **multi-venue routing only**.

When a single venue cannot fill the full order size — because its orderbook is too thin — Lotus can combine depth from multiple venues simultaneously. The extra shares filled beyond what any single venue could supply represent share improvement.

This is different from price improvement. A user can receive share improvement on an order even when the blended price is the same as the single-venue price, simply because multi-venue routing made more of the order fillable.

## The single-venue baseline

Both improvement types reference a committed baseline.

For price improvement, the baseline is the best safe single-venue route available at preview time — its average price.

For share improvement, the baseline is `singleVenueMaxFillSize`: the maximum shares the best single venue could fill at quote time. This value is committed when the quote is accepted so it can be independently verified.

If `singleVenueMaxFillSize` equals the full order size, no share improvement applies — a single venue could have filled everything.

## Price improvement formula

For buys:

```text
Q_ref = notional / referenceAveragePrice
Q_exec = filled shares from Lotus route
surplusShares = max(0, Q_exec - Q_ref)
grossPriceImprovement = surplusShares * executedAveragePrice
```

For sells:

```text
referenceProceeds = shares * referenceAveragePrice
executedProceeds = proceeds from Lotus route
grossPriceImprovement = max(0, executedProceeds - referenceProceeds)
```

## Share improvement formula

```text
extraShares = max(0, Q_exec - singleVenueMaxFillSize)
shareImprovementValue = extraShares * executedAveragePrice
shareImprovementFee = shareImprovementValue * shareImprovementShareBps / 10000
```

The user receives all the extra shares. Lotus captures only a configured percentage of their USDC value. The user keeps the rest.

## Estimated versus final numbers

Estimated improvement appears at preview time.

Final improvement is calculated after:

- Venue submit.
- Fill confirmation with actual execution prices.
- Fee accounting.

These numbers can differ because markets move between quote and fill.

## How Lotus makes money

Lotus captures a configured share of positive price improvement:

```text
priceImprovementFee = min(
  grossPriceImprovement * priceImprovementShareBps / 10000,
  notional * maxTotalFeeBpsOfNotional / 10000
)
```

Lotus also captures a configured share of share improvement value at execution time:

```text
shareImprovementFee = shareImprovementValue * shareImprovementShareBps / 10000
```

If a builder fee is configured, it follows the same pattern on price improvement:

```text
builderFee = min(
  grossPriceImprovement * builderShareBps / 10000,
  notional * maxBuilderFeeBpsOfNotional / 10000
)
```

The user-retained price improvement is:

```text
userRetainedPriceImprovement = grossPriceImprovement - priceImprovementFee - builderFee
```

If `userRetainedPriceImprovement` is not positive enough under the active policy, Lotus should not present the route as having price savings.

Share improvement fees are charged separately and do not affect the price improvement user-positive check — the user is always better off for receiving extra shares.

## Receipt fields

The final receipt should include:

- Reference average price.
- Executed average price.
- Gross price improvement.
- Price improvement fee.
- Share improvement — extra shares and fee, if any.
- Builder fee, if any.
- User-retained price improvement.
- Fee policy version.
- Settlement verification result.

## Common mistakes

- Do not calculate surplus against a stale route.
- Do not count unreachable liquidity in the reference route.
- Do not show estimated surplus as final settlement.
- Do not reward a route that worsens the user's net price outcome.
- Do not count share improvement as price improvement — they are different fee types with different timing.

<Note>
  Surplus capture aligns the protocol, builders, and users around better execution. Lotus only earns when execution genuinely improves the outcome.
</Note>
