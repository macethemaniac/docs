title: "Reading route details"
description: "How to audit why Lotus selected a route."
---

Route details explain how Lotus chose an execution path.

They are useful for builders and advanced traders who want to inspect the route instead of only seeing the simplified preview.

![Routing decision flow](/images/learn/routing-decision-flow.svg)

## What route details include

A route detail view can include:

- Canonical market and outcome.
- Side and amount.
- Venue preference.
- Eligible venues.
- Selected route type.
- Per-venue price and depth.
- Expected average price.
- Fees and estimated slippage.
- Savings versus the reference route.
- Readiness and blocker summaries.

Execution still requires fresh checks at place time.

## How to read venue legs

Each leg should show:

- Venue name.
- Venue market ID.
- Venue outcome ID or token ID.
- Side.
- Size.
- Expected price.
- Readiness status.

For sells, the executable token ID matters. If Lotus cannot derive the sell token ID, the route should not be executable.

## Route type

The route type tells you how many venues are used:

- `SINGLE`: one venue.
- `PAIR`: two venues.
- `TRI`: three venues.

More venues are not automatically better. The selected route should beat the simpler routes after fees, slippage, and risk.

## Common mistakes

- Do not use route details as proof that execution already happened.
- Do not assume an old route is still executable.
- Do not ignore missing venue outcome IDs.
- Do not compare routes without accounting for fees and depth.

<Note>
  Route details are audit data. They explain the decision, but submit still needs live execution gates.
</Note>
