---
title: "Understanding savings"
description: "How Lotus calculates route improvement."
---

Savings is the improvement Lotus expects from the selected route compared with a reference route.

The reference is usually the best safe single-venue route for the same market, outcome, side, and amount.

## What savings includes

Savings should account for:

- Venue price.
- Available depth.
- Fees.
- Expected slippage.
- Route size.
- Multi-leg split quality.
- Any builder or protocol fee shown in the preview.

Lotus should report estimated savings before execution and final savings after settlement verification.

## Why savings can change

Estimated savings can change because markets move.

The final receipt may differ from the preview if:

- A venue book moved before execution.
- A fallback route was used.
- One leg filled at a different level.
- Fees changed the final output.
- The order only partially filled under the selected policy.

The receipt should make this clear.

## What good savings looks like

Good savings are not only a green number.

A good route should be:

- Better than the reference route.
- Executable with fresh venue data.
- Safe under the shared outcome core.
- Supported by venue submit and settlement paths.
- Clear enough for the user to audit later.

## Common mistakes

- Do not compare against a stale baseline.
- Do not count theoretical depth that cannot be submitted.
- Do not treat savings as final before settlement verification.
- Do not choose a complex route for tiny or uncertain improvement.

<Note>
  Savings are useful only when the route is also executable and verifiable.
</Note>
