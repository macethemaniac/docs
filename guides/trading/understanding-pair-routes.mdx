title: "Understanding pair routes"
description: "How Lotus decides when two venues are better than one."
---

A pair route splits one order across two venues.

Lotus uses this when two venues give a better executable result than the best single venue after fees, slippage, depth, and route risk.

![Routing decision flow](/images/learn/routing-decision-flow.svg)

## What you see

In the trade preview, a pair route should appear as one simple order.

You may see:

- The route type: `PAIR`.
- The two venues used.
- The expected average price.
- Estimated savings versus a single-venue route.
- Any required signature step.

You do not need to place two separate orders.

## Why Lotus uses two venues

One venue can have the best visible price but not enough depth for the full order.

For example, the first venue may sell 100 shares cheaply, then become expensive. A second venue may have better depth for the next part of the trade. Splitting across both can improve the average fill.

Lotus compares the blended pair route against the best single venue before choosing it.

## What Lotus checks

Before showing a pair route as executable, Lotus checks:

- Both venues map to the same canonical market and outcome.
- Both legs have fresh live quotes.
- Both legs have enough depth for the intended size.
- Rule compatibility is safe.
- Funding or sellable position is ready.
- Any required signer or venue setup is ready.
- The final blended result improves the route.

If one leg fails these checks, Lotus should not force the pair route.

## Common mistakes

- Do not assume two venues are always better. Pair routes only help when the math improves.
- Do not compare only the top visible price. Depth matters more for larger orders.
- Do not treat display prices as execution authority. Execution still needs a fresh quote.

<Note>
  A pair route is selected for execution quality, not for visual venue count.
</Note>
