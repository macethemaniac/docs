Lotus gives you a single execution surface across prediction market venues. This guide covers connecting a wallet, getting a route quote, and placing your first trade.

<Note>
  Lotus is currently in private access. Reach out at [hi@uselotus.xyz](mailto:hi@uselotus.xyz) to request access.
</Note>

## Step 1: Connect a wallet

Lotus requires a wallet to sign orders and hold prediction market positions.

Supported signing methods:

- **EIP-712 (MetaMask, WalletConnect, hardware wallets)** — standard for Polymarket-compatible routes.
- **Managed wallets via Turnkey** — embedded wallet provisioned by Lotus for users who prefer not to manage keys directly.

Go to the Lotus app and follow the wallet connection prompt. For API integrations, see [Authentication](/integrate/authentication).

## Step 2: Fund your account

Prediction market positions require USDC collateral deposited at the relevant venue.

Lotus checks venue funding readiness before accepting an order. If your account is not funded at the target venue, the route will be blocked before submission.

See [Funding overview](/guides/funding/overview) for deposit flows per venue.

## Step 3: Get a route quote

Lotus prices routes live before you commit.

### In the app

1. Search for a market.
2. Pick an outcome and side (Buy or Sell).
3. Enter your size.
4. Lotus returns the best available route with estimated savings.

### Via API

```http
POST /execution/quote
Authorization: Bearer <token>

{
  "side": "buy",
  "marketId": "<canonical-market-id>",
  "outcomeId": "YES",
  "amount": "100",
  "candidates": [...]
}
```

The response includes:

- `routeType` — `SINGLE_VENUE`, `CROSS_VENUE` (pair or tri), or `RFQ_BACKED`.
- `expectedPrice` — the blended average execution price.
- `estimatedSavings` — improvement over the single-venue baseline, if any.
- `singleVenueMaxFillSize` — the maximum shares available from the best single venue alone. If the route fills beyond this, the extra shares came from multi-venue routing.
- `legs` — per-venue allocations, sizes, and prices.
- `requiredUserSignatureSteps` — which legs need a wallet signature before submit.

See [Execution API reference](/integrate/api-reference/execution) for the full schema.

## Step 4: Place the order

If the route requires no signature, it submits immediately. If a signature is needed:

1. Lotus returns a `signatureRequests` array.
2. Sign each request with your wallet.
3. Submit the signed bundle.

```http
POST /execution/{executionId}/submit-signed-bundle
Authorization: Bearer <token>

{
  "signedLegs": [...]
}
```

Lotus then submits each leg to the relevant venue and tracks the fill.

## Step 5: Track execution

After submission, check the status of your order:

```http
GET /execution/orders/{orderId}/status
Authorization: Bearer <token>
```

Possible states:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `SUBMITTED` | Sent to venue, awaiting fill confirmation. |
| `FILLED` | All legs filled. Settlement verification in progress. |
| `FAILED` | Submission or fill failed. See blockers. |
| `NEEDS_SIGNATURE` | Waiting for wallet signature before submit. |

## Step 6: View your positions

Once filled and settled, your position appears in your portfolio:

```http
GET /position
Authorization: Bearer <token>
```

Positions are unified across venues. A Polymarket YES share and a Limitless YES share on the same market appear as one position.

## What happens behind the scenes

Every Lotus order goes through a consistent flow:

1. Market discovery and normalization across venues.
2. Route scoring — price, depth, fees, slippage, rule compatibility.
3. Execution scope check — the route must be approved for the selected lane.
4. Submission to venue adapters per leg.
5. Fill tracking and settlement verification.
6. Surplus accounting — price improvement and share improvement calculated from verified fills.
7. Position and revenue ledger updated.
