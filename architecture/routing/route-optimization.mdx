---
title: "Route optimization"
description: "How Lotus scores candidate routes."
---

Route optimization chooses the best safe candidate from route discovery.

The goal is not to maximize venue count. The goal is to maximize the user's executable outcome after fees, slippage, and risk.

## Buy scoring

For a buy, the route should maximize expected shares:

```text
expectedShares = notional / expectedAveragePrice
```

The score then accounts for costs and risk:

```text
netBuyScore = expectedShares
  - feeImpact
  - slippagePenalty
  - executionRiskPenalty
```

## Sell scoring

For a sell, the route should maximize expected proceeds:

```text
expectedProceeds = shares * expectedAveragePrice
```

Then:

```text
netSellScore = expectedProceeds
  - feeImpact
  - slippagePenalty
  - executionRiskPenalty
```

## Pair and tri thresholds

Pair and tri routes should clear a minimum improvement threshold over the best simpler route.

```text
improvementBps = (selectedNetOutcome - baselineNetOutcome)
  / baselineNetOutcome
  * 10000
```

If the improvement is too small, Lotus should prefer the simpler route.

## Tiebreakers

When routes are close, Lotus should prefer:

1. Fewer legs.
2. Stronger settlement support.
3. Better venue readiness.
4. Lower slippage risk.
5. Clearer receipt and reconciliation path.

## Output

The optimizer outputs one selected route plus enough data to explain why it won.

That output feeds execution preview and route details.
